# project's name (50 characters)
IR power switch for NucBox G5

# summarize your project (140 characters)
Add IR power switch to a mini PC to turn on it with a TV remote.

# describe your project (1000 characters)
I am going to use this tiny PC to watch netflix on my living room.
So it would be handy if I am able to power on the PC with a TV remote.
This project aims to achieve it without compromising compactness of the PC.

# Details
<p>In mini PCs, power switch is soldered to a board directly and I need custom solution.</p>

<p>What I need to do is simple:</p>

<ol>
  <li>Find out which pin is the pin to turn on and attach a cable to it to connect to a mcu.</li>
  <li>Find out what can be used to power mcu.</li>
  <li>Connect IR receiver to mcu.</li>
  <li>Program mcu to output low to power switch pin when it received particular remote control signal.</li>
</ol>

## Find out pin assignments of the power switch
<p>First of all, I need to know which pins should I short to turn on the PC.</p>

<p>
  I opened case and checked pin assignments by measuring voltage and resistance.<br>
  Result is in the following picture:
</p>

<!-- nucbox-g5-power-pinassign.jpg -->
<figure><img src="https://cdn.hackaday.io/images/2319321752579865126.jpg" data-image="1752579865632" width="400" height="400"></figure>

<p>
  First pin, left most one, outputs 3.3V even if system is shut down. This seems to powering LED.<br>
  Second pin is connected to ground.<br>
  Third is the one I need. Shorting it to ground turns on the PC.<br>
  Forth pin controls the LED. Shorting it to ground turns on the LED.<br>
  In the other words, outer pins are for LED, inner pins are for switch.
</p>

<p>
  It turned out there is everything I need: power, ground and switch. Additionaly, I may be able to use LED control pin to detect whether PC is on or off.<br>
  I don't know how much power I can draw from the 3.3V source, but it is unlikely to hurt anything if I draw a few milliamperes.
</p>
